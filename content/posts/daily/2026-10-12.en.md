## Unified Systems View reading

Two items in this issue concern calculation and measurement respectively: a paper gives a closed-form expression for the logical failure rate of the toric code [1]; NASA plans to collect performance data on heat-shield materials during capsule reentry [4].

**Item 1: a way to directly calculate the logical failure rate of the toric code.** Background: on 2026-10-06 PRX Quantum published a paper that maps the logical failure rate (the probability of still getting an error after error correction) of the toric code (an error-correcting code that uses topological structure to protect quantum information) exactly onto the 2D Ising model (a classic statistical-mechanics model of many interacting small units), obtaining a closed-form expression (a result that can be written down directly as a formula) [1]. What changed: the abstract says this provides analytic tools for benchmarking topological codes beyond current computational limits [1]. Worth watching: whether this method is used to benchmark other topological codes.

**Item 2: A paper traces the electric origin of thermal frequency shifts in a silicon hole spin.** Background: on 2026-10-07 PRX Quantum published a paper on a single silicon hole spin (the spin state shown by a hole formed by a missing electron in silicon, which can serve as a qubit) [2]. The issue: the silicon hole spin studied in the paper shows thermally induced frequency shifts [2]. What changed: the paper reports that this frequency shift comes from an electric effect and identifies a magnetic-field operating point immune to it; the abstract says this can guide the design of semiconductor qubits that are more robust to thermal effects [2]. Worth watching: whether follow-up experiments are published that run qubits at this operating point.

**Item 3: NASA seeks plans for commercial space stations.** Background: on 2026-10-09 NASA issued a news release saying it had released its final Request for Proposals (a formal document in which the government invites companies to submit plans), asking industry to submit plans for the next generation of commercial space stations [3]. Why it matters: the reason the release gives is to maintain a sustained presence in low Earth orbit after the International Space Station retires [3]. Related steps: in March this year NASA published two Requests for Information, in July a draft Request for Proposals, followed by an industry day [3]. Proposals are due on December 8, 2026; contract awards are worth watching next [3].

**Item 4: testing heat shields by "taking out the trash".** Background: on 2026-10-09 NASA published news saying a cargo spacecraft leaving the International Space Station carried 12 small experimental capsules to test next-generation thermal protection materials, with the aim of developing heat shields for astronauts returning from the Moon and Mars [4]. What changed: NASA has put experimental capsules on a departing cargo spacecraft and plans to collect data during reentry on how heat-shield materials perform [4]; the experiment, called KREPE-3, is the third in a series of low-cost missions [4]. Worth watching: whether the measurements from these 12 capsules are published.

**Item 5: SpaceX reports a Falcon 9 launch.** Background: SpaceX's posts report the rocket's liftoff and the first-stage landing respectively, and say that Falcon 9 flew a data transport mission to low Earth orbit for @SemperCitiusSDA [9][10][11]. Too little information yet; no framework reading for now.
